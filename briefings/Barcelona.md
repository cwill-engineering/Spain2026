## The 60-Second Orientation

Barcelona is the capital of **Catalonia** (Catalunya) — 1.6 million in the city, 5.7 million in the metro — and the most economically powerful region in Spain. It is also the place where many Spaniards feel least "Spanish." Catalans run their own schools (Catalan-first education), police (Mossos d'Esquadra), health system, and broadcast media. The independence referendum of **October 2017** failed legally, succeeded emotionally, and still defines the mood.

For you, Barcelona is wedding terrain: the **Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic)** is Roman, medieval, and tourist-saturated all at once. Your Eixample apartment sits on **Carrer de València** — not Valencia the city, but a grid street named after the city you just left. Welcome to Cerdà's 19th-century urban machine.

Barcelona makes money from **port, industry, tourism, and tech** — in that rough historical order. What changed everything visible: **1880s–1910s Modernisme** (Gaudí era), **1992 Olympics** (waterfront + global brand), and **2010s tourism explosion** (love/hate).

## Deep History: War, Conquest, and Turning Points

**Barcino (Roman colony).** Founded under Emperor Augustus. The Roman wall defines the Gothic Quarter's shape — you can still touch it in the MUHBA museum.

**Visigoths, then Moors (8th century).** Brief; Barcelona's story is really **Catalan + Mediterranean**, not Andalusian.

**County of Barcelona → Crown of Aragon (801–1469).** Barcelona became the maritime hub of an empire stretching to Naples, Sicily, Athens. Medieval wealth built the Gothic churches you walk past for the wedding. Catalán was a language of kings and merchants, not peasants.

**Union with Castile (1469).** Ferdinand of Aragon married Isabella of Castile — Spain as we know it. Barcelona's commercial elite lost primacy to Seville and later Madrid.

**1714 — The Siege.** War of Spanish Succession. Barcelona backed the Habsburgs. Bourbon victory on **11 September** (still Catalonia's national day — *Diada*) meant loss of institutions, banning of Catalan in administration, construction of the **Ciutadella fortress** to watch the conquered city. This date is tattooed on regional identity.

**Industrial 19th century.** Textiles, workers' movements, anarchism — Barcelona was the most radical city in Spain pre–Civil War.

**1888 / 1929 Expos.** Urban expansion, Montjuïc developed, Modernisme flourished.

**Spanish Civil War (1936–1939).** Barcelona was anarchist/republican heartland until Franco's conquest in January 1939. Bombings, purges, then **40 years of Franco**: Catalan banned in public life, street names Castilianized, FC Barcelona as covert national symbol.

**Democracy (1978 onward).** Autonomy restored. **1992 Olympics** — the beach you see did not exist as leisure space; it was industrial port. The Games rewrote the coastline and global image.

**2017 independence crisis.** Referendum, police violence, leaders jailed or exiled, amnesty law years later (2024). Movement is quieter now but **identity politics is ambient noise**, not a settled issue.

**Post-COVID tourism wars.** "Tourists go home" graffiti, limits on short-term rentals, water protests — Barcelona is actively managing its own success.

## Politics Now

**Catalonia (regional).** Governed by a shifting coalition — since 2024, **Salvador Illa** (PSC, Catalan wing of PSOE) as president after years of pro-independence majorities. Independence parties (ERC, Junts) still matter but lack 2017 momentum. **Language**: Catalan dominant in schools and official life; ~50%+ of metro population born outside Catalonia — integration friction is constant.

**Barcelona city.** Mayor **Jaume Collboni** (PSC) from 2024, replacing **Ada Colau** (Barcelona en Comú, left-green). Colau fought tourism and superblocks; Collboni signals **pragmatism** — keep economy humming, manage housing. Short-term rental crackdowns continue.

**Spain (national).** Sánchez needs Catalan party votes in Madrid's Congress — gives Catalonia leverage on amnesty, funding, infrastructure. **Amnesty law (2024)** and Constitutional Court review (2026) remain emotionally raw: for supporters, healing; for opponents, rule-of-law betrayal.

**Right wing.** PP + **Vox** coalition possible in future — Vox is especially strong where immigration and Catalan identity collide.

**Street level:** Senyera (Catalan flag) on balconies; **estelada** (starred independence flag) less common than 2017 but still visible; Spanish flags often deliberate counter-signal.

## Demographics, Economy, and Cultural Shifts

**Barcelona metro (~5.7M):**
- **~22% foreign-born** — Latin America, Morocco, Pakistan, Romania largest groups
- Low birth rate; growth from immigration
- **Tourism**: pre-COVID ~30M overnight stays/year — crushing for old city infrastructure
- **Unemployment** historically higher than Madrid; youth job quality a grievance

**Economy:** Port of Barcelona is Mediterranean top tier. Industry (chemicals, autos) in belt around city. **Tech/startups** in 22@ and Poblenou. Fashion, design, medical research. Wedding tourism is niche but yours is part of a bigger events economy.

**Shifts you'll feel:**
- **Housing crisis** — locals vs Airbnb (your VRBO is part of the debate)
- **Superblocks (superilles)** — pedestrian priority pilots; some love, some hate
- **Water stress** — Mediterranean drought politics; don't waste tap water loudly
- **Shift from independence to "liveability"** — younger Catalans often prioritize rent over flags

## Local News (Spring 2026)

**Collboni's war on tourist flats (your VRBO is in the crosshairs).** Mayor **Jaume Collboni** (PSC) pledged to **revoke all ~10,000 licensed tourist apartment permits by November 2028** — Europe's toughest anti-Airbnb move. Existing bookings remain legal; the fight is about whether Barcelona should keep hosting visitors like you or force units back to long-term rentals. Opposition says the city will lose **Primavera Sound**, MWC, and 40,000 jobs; Collboni says housing is existential. **April 2026:** general tourist tax increased again — hotels ~€10–17/night, vacation rentals ~€14/night. **For you:** your Eixample VRBO is legal now; understand locals may side-eye short-term guests. Do not debate Airbnb at the wedding bar unless invited.

**Cruise tax doubled — fast-tracked.** Collboni pushed to **double the overnight cruise passenger tax** from €4 to €8 immediately (not phased over four years). Goal: discourage day-tripping cruise crowds that swamp the Rambla without spending locally. Terminal count cut from seven to five. **For you:** minimal direct impact; explains anti-tourism graffiti.

**Primavera Sound (Jun 3–7) loading in.** One of Europe's biggest indie festivals sets up at **Parc del Fòrum** right after you leave Jun 1. By May 30–31 you may see freight, fencing, and extra transit near the waterfront. **200,000+** accommodation nights historically — city already in festival-season metabolism.

**Catalan amnesty law — Constitutional Court (Feb 2026).** Spain's TC issued a complex ruling on the **2024 amnesty** for 2017 independence process defendants — partial loss of object, constitutional questions alive. Catalonia watches whether reconciliation or betrayal wins. Street temperature: lower than 2017, but **identity is not settled**.

**Housing = national spring storyline.** Madrid march May 24; Barcelona's left (Comuns) wants **€50M diverted from tourism promotion to housing**. Slogan in streets: *"pisos per viure-hi, no per especular"* — homes to live in, not to speculate.

**Sagrada completion timeline.** Still no end date — church generates more tourism revenue unfinished than many finished cathedrals. Your May 30 entry is peak bucket-list season; expect security lines even with tickets.
