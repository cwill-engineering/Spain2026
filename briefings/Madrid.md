## The 60-Second Orientation

Madrid is the **capital of Spain** and the **Comunidad de Madrid** region — ~3.3 million in the city, ~6.8 million in the metro. Unlike Barcelona or Valencia, it has **no Roman origin story and no port**. Philip II made it capital in **1561** because it was geographically central and politically neutral — a blank slate for imperial administration.

Today Madrid is where **money, government, media, and monarchy** concentrate. The king does not live in the big palace anymore, but the machinery of state surrounds you. Your Airbnb in **La Latina** puts you in pre-tourist Madrid — market streets, taverns, churches — ten minutes from **Puerta del Sol** (kilometer zero of Spain's road network) and **Plaza Mayor**.

Madrid runs **one hour behind Valencia/Barcelona** in political temperament: more Castilian, more national, less regionalist — but **Madrid region** has its own brand of conservative autonomy under **Isabel Díaz Ayuso** (PP), often at war with Sánchez's national government.

## Deep History: War, Conquest, and Turning Points

**Pre-1561.** Small Moorish outpost (Mayrit = water channels). Castilian frontier town. No grand empire yet.

**1561 — Philip II moves the court.** Madrid becomes capital of the **Spanish Empire** at its zenith — silver from the Americas flows through Seville/Cádiz, decisions through Madrid. The **Palacio Real** (still Europe's largest royal palace by floor area) and monastic network expand.

**1700s — Bourbon Madrid.** Philip V and successors impose French-style urban planning — **Salón del Prado**, neoclassical ministries, rational boulevards.

**1808 — Dos de Mayo uprising.** Madrid rose against Napoleon's occupying forces — brutal reprisal, painted by Goya (*The Third of May 1808* in the Prado). Modern Spanish nationalism has a martyrdom date here too.

**Spanish Civil War (1936–1939).** Madrid **held out under siege** until the end — iconic republican resistance. Franco entered victorious; repression followed. Madrid swelled with rural migrants under Franco — castellanization, industrial belts, conservative Catholic culture.

**Democracy (1978 onward).** Explosive growth — **La Movida Madrileña** (late 70s–80s counterculture), EU membership, immigration boom, skyline rise in **AZCA / Cuatro Torres**.

**2004 Atocha bombings.** Islamist terrorist attack on commuter trains — 193 dead. National trauma; Atocha station (where your AVE arrives) is also a memorial site.

**2008 financial crisis.** Youth unemployment catastrophe; **Indignados (15-M) movement 2011** born in Madrid — precursor to global Occupy. Still shapes housing politics.

**COVID and after.** Tourism rebound; short-term rental fights; heat-island summers pushing 40°C by July (you miss the worst in early June).

## Politics Now

**Spain (national).** Government in Madrid physically — Moncloa Palace, Cortes (Congress/Senate). **Sánchez** coalition fragile; opposition **PP + Vox** attacks on immigration, amnesty, energy policy. **2027 election** looms.

**Comunidad de Madrid (region).** **Isabel Díaz Ayuso (PP)** — high-profile, media-savvy, culture-warrior brand ("Madrid freedom" vs COVID lockdowns, low taxes narrative). Won landslides. Model for PP nationally. Clashes constantly with national left on health, housing, environment.

**Madrid city.** Mayor **José Luis Martínez-Almeida (PP)**. Cleaner than Ayuso rhetorically; same party. Focus on **events, tourism, mobility** — Madrid wants to be Barcelona's rival for conventions and nightlife.

**Monarchy.** King **Felipe VI** — low-key post-Juan Carlos scandals; monarchy accepted but not universally loved. Republican sentiment exists, quieter than Catalan independence.

**Street politics:** Housing protests (banners for rent control), taxi vs Uber fights, immigrant-rights marches — Madrid is where national debates hit pavement.

## Demographics, Economy, and Cultural Shifts

**Metro ~6.8M:**
- **~17% foreign-born** — Latin America largest share (Colombia, Venezuela, Peru), plus Moroccan, Romanian communities
- **Aging slower than rural Spain** — immigration keeps workforce young
- **Internal migration** from all regions — Madrid is Spain's melting pot

**Economy:** Finance, telecom, government, tourism, universities, healthcare. **No port** — Madrid sells services and power. Higher GDP per capita than national average. **Unemployment** improved post-COVID but youth still precarious.

**Cultural shifts:**
- **Late-night economy** — dinner 22:00, bars midnight, norm not exception
- **Secularization** — church attendance low except holidays (Corpus Christi still matters institutionally)
- **Football** — Real Madrid as global brand; Atlético as local grit identity
- **Rent crisis** — La Latina gentrifying; your short stay is in a desirable zone

## Local News (Spring 2026)

**"La vivienda nos cuesta la vida" — housing spring (May 24).** Dozens of groups including **Sindicato de Inquilinas** marched **Sunday May 24, 12:00 from Atocha** along Paseo del Prado → Alcalá → Metro Sevilla. Organizers claim rents up **50%+** in a decade; demand rent controls, end to abusive contracts, more public housing. Part of a **national "hot spring"** of housing demos (Guadalajara May 23, Madrid May 24, more to follow). **For you:** you arrive Jun 1 — protests done, but **posters, banners, and bitterness remain**. Your La Latina Airbnb sits in a neighborhood locals fear is pricing them out.

**Ayuso vs Metro Line 7B victims (May 22 ruling).** **El País** reported a court ordered **Comunidad de Madrid** to pay **€89,608 more** than offered to a couple whose home was demolished for **Metro Line 7B** construction in San Fernando de Henares — subsidence destroyed 73 buildings. President **Isabel Díaz Ayuso** had called earlier payouts "maximum legal." ~30 more families expect similar rulings — **million-euro liability** for regional government. **For you:** emblematic of Ayuso's "low taxes, fast growth" model colliding with courts; explains why she fights national housing regulation.

**New Madrid housing law (effective June 2026).** Regional government approved accelerated **social housing construction** — minister **Jorge Rodrigo**: *"build housing, build housing, and build housing."* Critics say it ignores rent caps; supporters say supply is the only fix. Debate mirrors national PP vs PSOE split you will feel in bar conversations.

**Corpus Christi (Jun 4 — YOUR checkout day).** Public holiday in Madrid and Castile. **Religious processions** near Almudena Cathedral and center; many **shops and banks closed**. Not a festival atmosphere — solemn, family, church-centric. **Mercado de San Miguel** may run reduced hours. **Plan:** stock breakfast night before; confirm taxi Jun 3; eat lunch early Jun 4 before airport.

**National context landing on Madrid:** Sánchez coalition survival, amnesty law court fight, **2027 election** positioning — Madrid media (El País, El Mundo, OK Diario) is the megaphone for all of it. Walk past **Moncloa** or **Congreso** and you are near the fault lines.

**Heat watch:** Early June can hit **30°C+**; 2026 spring has been warm. Retiro has shade; plan museum mornings.
